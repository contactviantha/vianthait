Swiss Banking and WealthTech API Development from India

Swiss private banks and wealth managers ask us harder questions than rate cards. Here is what they want to know about FINMA expectations, SIC payments and outsourcing engineering work to India.

The question Swiss teams put to us on the first call is narrower: do you know what FINMA expects from an onboarding flow, and will you treat a family office's data with the discretion our licence requires?

What Swiss Firms Actually Hand to an Offshore Team

The work that survives Swiss procurement has edges you can point at: an API contract, an authentication flow, defined failure states, and test cases that pass or fail without interpretation. Three areas dominate.

Onboarding and KYC

Swiss onboarding has to satisfy customer due diligence under the AML Act, FinSA disclosure duties and the bank's own risk committee. That is where a FINMA KYC compliance software developer earns the fee: wiring document capture, liveness and sanctions screening vendors, handling the pending and manual-review states, and writing the audit trail your compliance officer pulls in the next review. The engineering is ordinary; the evidence trail is not. That work is set out on our API integration page.

Wealth and portfolio reporting

Most Switzerland wealth management API integration work is pulling custodian and fund feeds into a portfolio system, normalising valuations and transactions, and producing numbers that still hold up when a family office asks how they were calculated. Multi-currency positions and corporate actions are where offshore teams with no capital markets background fall behind quietly.

Payments and SIC

A competent SIC payment API integration developer will talk cut-off times, ISO 20022 structure, status reporting and reconciliation before you mention your own stack. SIC is the Swiss Interbank Clearing system for low-value payments, and the failure modes are mundane: an instruction rejected hours after acceptance, a duplicate that got through because nobody set an idempotency key, a reconciliation job nobody runs.

FINMA, Secrecy and Where Your Data Is Allowed to Sit

FINMA is the Swiss financial regulator. It supervises banks, insurers and market infrastructure, and it does not certify your software vendor. Accountability for data, conduct and reporting stays with your institution, which means your offshore team's habits become part of your audit evidence.

Practically this shows up as data location expectations, strict access control, no production personal data in development, and documented deletion. Swiss institutions often want client data kept in Switzerland, or its location written into the contract. The revised Federal Act on Data Protection tightened processing records and breach notification, and GDPR applies whenever European clients are in scope.

One thing worth budgeting honestly: compliance reviews take longer than anyone plans for. A two-week security questionnaire becomes five weeks once legal and the risk committee are both involved. Put that in the timeline now rather than negotiating it in month three.

Switzerland's Open Finance Discussions and What You Can Build Today

There is no PSD2 equivalent in Switzerland, and any vendor who tells you otherwise is reading the wrong country's legislation. What exists is a mix of industry commitments and Switzerland's open finance discussions, including the 2019 voluntary agreement between established banks and fintech firms on API access.

An Open Finance Switzerland API developer should be honest about what that means for your roadmap. There is no mandated timeline to build against, so build for the regime that may arrive: a standardised consent model, a clean access audit trail, an API surface a stricter future rule could plug into. Vendors who promise mandate dates usually have not read the papers.

Why Zurich and Geneva Firms Outsource to India

Most Swiss fintech outsource India enquiries start with a rate comparison. What keeps the arrangement alive is retention: the engineer who learned your custodian feed quirks in month one is still there in month nine, because our team sits in Trichy, where churn is far below Bangalore or Hyderabad.

The profile we most often field is a private banking software developer India teams have already worked with on reporting or onboarding. Domain familiarity matters more than headcount here: when someone has seen a family office onboarding flow before, the first meeting starts at your problem instead of a glossary.

Language deserves a straight answer. English is fine for engineering, and every technical conversation runs in it. Stakeholder documents are a different matter: risk committee papers and client notices in German or French need a native reviewer, and no vendor should pretend otherwise. Our handover documents are written to be translated.

Swiss buyers weigh discretion more heavily than price, which is fair, and it shows up in small things: who joins the call, what gets written down, whether your vendor discusses other clients. If you would rather discuss it directly, send us your project outline.

Engagement, Hours and How the Engagement Actually Runs

India Standard Time runs 4.5 hours ahead of Zurich in winter and 3.5 in summer, so a 9:00 start in Zurich lands mid-afternoon here. We agree a fixed overlap window in writing at the start: decisions and reviews inside it, deep work either side.

The usual shape of an engagement:

  1. Week 1, technical discovery. Our engineer and yours walk the system. You get a written scope with assumptions listed.
  2. Weeks 2 to 4, paid pilot. One integration, delivered into your repository on your infrastructure.
  3. Month 2 onward, dedicated team. Same engineers, wider scope, weekly demo instead of status emails.

Everything runs in your repository and your project tool. If the pilot grows, the wider service catalogue is there.

Questions That Separate a Real Vendor From a Profile

You can usually tell within one conversation. Ask these four and watch the detail level:

  • "Walk me through a SIC payment that is accepted and then rejected." If the answer skips status polling and reconciliation, they have not shipped Swiss payments.
  • "Where will our data physically sit, and how do you record that?" Vague answers become contract problems later.
  • "How do you handle stakeholder documents that need German or French?" You want an honest boundary, not a promise to translate everything.
  • "Can we start with a two to four week paid pilot?" Refusal, or a push for a long commitment first, tells you where the incentives sit.

If your team would rather show us the system than run an interrogation, that works too. Our work for banks and lenders is described on the banking technology page.

FAQs Swiss Clients Ask Us

Which Indian company builds Swiss banking API software?

Viantha Solutions does, from Trichy in Tamil Nadu with headquarters in Singapore: KYC and onboarding flows, wealth and portfolio reporting, SIC payment integration and open finance readiness on a Spring Boot, React and AWS stack.

What kind of work does a Swiss fintech outsource to India first?

Usually one integration with clear edges: a KYC flow, a reporting API or a payment connection. A two to four week paid pilot on that scope comes first, with the wider commitment decided on evidence rather than on a proposal deck.

How does FINMA compliance work with an offshore development team?

FINMA regulates your institution, not us, so accountability stays with you. We control the evidence: access logs, environment separation, no production personal data in development, documented deletion and commit history. We sign the NDA and data processing terms your counsel requires.

Do you handle German and French stakeholder documents?

Engineering runs in English and always has. For risk committee papers and client notices we write structured drafts for translation, and we tell you when a native German or French reviewer should sign off.
